# Anaplan — issuer memo

## One-line thesis
Anaplan is a **control case for connected planning software** in a universe of stressed names: Latham publicly documented direct-lender financing, multiple BDC filings confirm first-lien exposure at ARCC and BXSL, and current marks are clean — making Anaplan useful not as a current distress signal but as the baseline against which deterioration in weaker planning or workflow software credits should be measured.

## What the company does
Anaplan provides enterprise planning software used for finance, sales, supply-chain, and operational planning. Lenders can like the name because planning systems can be sticky and cross-functional: once a company builds financial models, headcount plans, and supply-chain scenarios inside Anaplan, switching platforms requires rebuilding deeply embedded analytical workflows.

That stickiness is also what makes connected planning software a useful calibration point. If Anaplan marks remain healthy while weaker categories (customer support, workflow automation, content management) deteriorate, it supports the view that software credit risk is a **dispersion story** — some subcategories are genuinely more defensible than others.

## Why it matters
- **confirmed direct-lender financing** at the Thoma Bravo take-private ($10.7 billion transaction, 2022), with Latham identifying direct lenders in the financing
- **filing-confirmed first-lien exposure** at ARCC and BXSL — two of the most watched public BDC vehicles
- clean current marks (near par across multiple filings) provide a useful baseline for comparing against stressed names
- planning software is sticky but valuation-sensitive, making it a useful test of whether sponsor-backed planning tools remain healthy as enterprise tech spending cycles shift

## Debt / financing timeline
- **2022:** Thoma Bravo acquires Anaplan in a transaction valued at approximately **$10.7 billion**.
- **2022:** Latham & Watkins publicly discloses advising **direct lenders on the financing** to support the Thoma Bravo acquisition of Anaplan — the first public confirmation that the deal used private-credit / direct-lending capital rather than broadly syndicated loans.
- **Q3–Q4 2025:** ARCC and BXSL both carry Anaplan first-lien positions in their filed schedules, at clean par or slight-premium marks, with no PIK or non-accrual flags.

## Capital-structure relevance
The Anaplan direct-lender financing was part of a broader wave of large Thoma Bravo take-privates financed through private credit in 2021–2022. The category (connected planning software) tends to be valued at high revenue multiples during growth periods and can see sharp multiple compression when:
- enterprise software budgets tighten
- organic growth slows below underwriting assumptions
- interest rates rise faster than planned, increasing debt-service burden relative to free cash flow

Anaplan was underwritten at peak 2022 software valuations. If planning-software multiples have since compressed, there may be a gap between the original underwriting EV and current recovery value — even if operations remain stable. This is the risk that does not appear in clean par marks today but could become relevant at refinancing in 2029.

## How to use Anaplan in the broader analysis
**As a control case:**
- If Anaplan marks remain near par while names like Medallia or Finastra deteriorate, it supports the view that the problem is category-specific, not generic to all software private credit
- Anaplan's clean marks give you a baseline for what "healthy software credit" looks like in public BDC disclosures

**As a latent risk to watch:**
- The June 2029 maturity creates a refinancing event that will reveal how the market values connected planning software in ~3 years
- If growth has slowed meaningfully, interest coverage has compressed, or leverage has not improved materially from closing, a 2029 refinancing could reveal a wider gap between marks and market-clearing value

## Hidden-mark-risk thesis
The mark risk is **not current** but **latent**. At par marks with no PIK or non-accrual flags, Anaplan looks like a healthy credit today. The latent risk is:
1. underwritten at peak 2022 multiples; the 2029 refinancing will reveal whether enterprise value has kept pace
2. planning software growth trajectories depend on enterprise budget cycles that may have softened post-2022
3. Thoma Bravo's playbook involves margin improvement and product consolidation, but if organic growth has slowed, exit multiples at refinancing time may be lower than the original underwriting assumed

## Working judgment
- **Hidden mark risk: 2 / 5** — current marks are clean and filing-confirmed at par; latent refinancing risk is real but not near-term
- **BDC bond relevance: 3 / 5** — useful control case; ARCC and BXSL confirmed; but not a current risk driver
- **Recovery fragility: 3 / 5** — planning software has real switching costs, but valuation sensitivity is high
