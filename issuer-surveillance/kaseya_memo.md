# Kaseya — issuer memo

## One-line thesis
Kaseya is a strong test of whether lenders are underestimating downside in **sticky-on-paper software platforms** where integration complexity, customer pushback, and capital-structure layering can matter more than the recurring-revenue label suggests — with ARCC's PIK preferred equity in the holding company adding a structural complexity signal not visible in the first-lien debt marks.

## What the company does
Kaseya provides IT-management, endpoint, security, backup, and automation software used heavily by managed service providers and internal IT teams. Lenders can like this profile because it appears to offer:
- deeply embedded operational workflows
- painful switching costs for MSP customers
- recurring software revenue
- infrastructure-like positioning rather than discretionary software spend
- consolidation opportunities under sponsor ownership

That is exactly why it is useful as a surveillance name. If this type of credit weakens, it suggests that private-credit marks may be too generous across a broader set of supposedly resilient software assets.

## Why it matters
- **$3.7 billion** in senior secured commitments for the 2022 Datto acquisition, with a publicly identified lender group including Golub, Blackstone Credit, Ares, Owl Rock, Oak Hill, Carlyle, and Sixth Street
- **ARCC holds a PIK preferred equity stake** in the holding company (Knockout Intermediate Holdings I) — Q3 2025: cost $31.8M at 14.62% PIK; Q4 2025: cost grew to $34.1M at 14.35% PIK (cost basis accretes as PIK compounds). A subordinated instrument that accrues but does not pay cash, signaling a complex capital structure designed to support the equity story
- 2025 refinancing attempt of approximately **$4 billion** is a key surveillance catalyst for how the market views the credit today
- the "sticky IT platform" narrative is exactly what private-credit underwriting finds most comfortable, making any weakness highly instructive for the broader software-credit book

## Debt / financing timeline
- **June 2022:** Kaseya announces and completes the **$6.2 billion** acquisition of Datto.
- **2022:** Paul, Weiss describes the debt financing as including **$3.7 billion of senior secured commitments**: a **$3.3 billion initial term loan**, **$200 million revolving credit facility**, and **$200 million delayed-draw term loan**, plus **$1 billion of preferred equity** at an indirect parent.
- **2022:** Paul, Weiss identifies the financing sources as including **Golub Capital, Blackstone Credit, Ares, Owl Rock, Oak Hill, Carlyle Global Credit, and Sixth Street**.
- **2025:** Public market reporting indicates a refinancing process of approximately **$4 billion**, including a first-lien / second-lien structure — the execution of which reveals where the market actually clears today.
- **Q3–Q4 2025:** CGBD and GSBD carry first-lien Kaseya exposure at slight premiums to par, suggesting the primary lender marks remain healthy. ARCC carries a PIK preferred equity stake in the holding company at cost.

**Key structural observation:** CGBD and GSBD hold first-lien debt marked at par or slight premium. ARCC holds **preferred equity in the holding company** accruing at **14.62% PIK** — a junior, non-cash-pay instrument that sits behind the first-lien debt. This structural complexity is consistent with a capital structure designed to defer equity returns while the business scales. It is also consistent with a structure that may be more levered at the holding company than the first-lien debt marks alone imply.

## Capital-structure relevance
Kaseya is useful because the story sits right in the zone where private-credit underwriting often sounds most comfortable:
- sticky product set for MSP workflows
- mission-critical use cases
- recurring revenue
- sponsor-backed scale
- clear acquisition logic (Datto added significant endpoint and backup capabilities)

If that kind of borrower still needs a more defensive refinancing or reveals weaker-than-expected economics, the implication is that software credit risk is broader than obviously troubled categories.

The ARCC preferred equity at 14.62% PIK warrants specific attention:
- at $31.8M cost basis, this instrument is accumulating interest without cash payments
- it sits structurally behind $3.7B+ of first-lien senior secured debt
- a holding-company preferred at this yield typically implies either a capital-structure optimization for tax or sponsor-return purposes, or a deeper subordinated risk layer than the surface debt marks reveal
- if the company struggles to de-lever, the preferred equity accruals compound the liability picture

## Hidden-mark-risk thesis
The likely blind spot is that a "sticky software" story can hide **integration strain, customer dissatisfaction, and slower deleveraging** until refinancing structure forces lenders to show what they really think. The Datto integration combined a large MSP-facing platform with Kaseya's existing product set, and integration complexity at scale can quietly pressure retention, R&D, and product coherence.

## Why it could matter to BDC unsecured bonds
For unsecured BDC bond investors, Kaseya matters as a **confidence transmission case** more than a one-name impairment story. If lenders are forced to refinance at weaker economics, accept layered structure, or leave difficult risk behind, it undermines the idea that software-service platforms are inherently safe first-lien assets.

## First transmission channel to BDC unsecured bond risk
The likely first transmission channel is **underwriting-confidence erosion**, via:
1. refinancing terms that reveal weaker market appetite than expected
2. pressure on marks for directly or ecosystem-exposed lenders
3. skepticism toward other consolidated software-platform credits
4. spread widening in BDC unsecured bonds if investors infer that software-portfolio marks are flattered by lagging recognition

## What to watch
- explicit 2025 refinancing structure and pricing relative to 2022 terms
- first-lien / second-lien split and whether any residual private-credit hold positions remain after syndication
- ARCC's PIK preferred equity: does it remain at cost, or do subsequent filings show a mark change?
- operating evidence: customer retention, integration progress, and deleveraging pace
- whether markets are refinancing the name because the credit is healthier or simply because loan windows reopened in a favorable market

## What would falsify or weaken the bearish view
The Kaseya concern would soften if we saw:
- clean refinancing execution with strong demand, tighter pricing, and no need for structural complexity
- evidence of stable customer retention across the combined Kaseya-Datto platform
- meaningful deleveraging below the 2022 closing leverage rather than refinancing in place
- ARCC's preferred equity receiving cash distributions, signaling the business is generating enough cash to service the entire stack

## Working judgment
- **Hidden mark risk: 4 / 5** — first-lien marks look healthy, but holding-company PIK preferred and integration complexity create meaningful blind spots
- **BDC bond relevance: 4 / 5** — confirmed CGBD, GSBD, and ARCC exposure; 2025 refinancing is a live catalyst
- **Recovery fragility: 4 / 5** — sticky MSP software with good switching costs, but integration risk and capital-structure layering create meaningful downside uncertainty
