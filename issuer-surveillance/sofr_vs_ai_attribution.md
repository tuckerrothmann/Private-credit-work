# SOFR Rate Shock vs. AI Disruption: Separating the Two Stress Drivers

*The "software AI thesis" is being applied too broadly. This document cleanly separates the two causal mechanisms driving private-credit software distress and assigns each surveillance name to its primary driver.*

---

## The two mechanisms

### Mechanism 1: SOFR rate shock on 2020–2021 vintage LBO debt

The 2020–2021 LBO cohort was underwritten at LIBOR rates of effectively zero (LIBOR averaged ~15bps in 2020–2021). The standard direct-lending spread was L+450–550bps, producing an effective all-in rate of approximately **5.0–5.5%** at origination. By Q4 2023, with SOFR at 5.30%+, the same debt was running at approximately **9.5–10.5%** — a near-doubling of cash interest burden within 18–24 months of origination.

**The math at scale:**
- $500M first-lien at L+450, 0.75% floor → Q4 2020 rate: ~5.25% → **$26M/year interest**
- Same instrument in Q4 2023: SOFR+450bps, floor irrelevant → ~9.75% → **$49M/year interest**
- **Incremental annual cash burden: ~$23M** — entirely unplanned in the original underwriting model

For a company with $50–60M EBITDA at LBO close (typical for a $500M EV transaction at 8–9x), adding $23M in unplanned interest expense turns 2.0x EBITDA coverage into ~1.5x. At $70–80M interest total, it turns 2.0x into 1.1x. This is a mechanical deterioration that has nothing to do with the operating company's competitive position.

### Mechanism 2: AI-driven operating deterioration

A separate category of names faces genuine revenue pressure from AI-native competitors replacing or compressing their core product value proposition. The mechanism is:
- AI-native tools (LLMs, copilots, autonomous agents) replicate specific workflows previously requiring SaaS tooling
- Enterprise buyers reduce seat counts or switch to cheaper AI-native alternatives
- NRR (net revenue retention) declines; renewal rates compress; pricing power erodes
- Revenue growth slows or reverses before lenders recognize the trend in marks

This is a fundamentally different stress pathway — operating performance deterioration driving enterprise value compression — rather than a financing cost shock on an otherwise healthy business.

## Key implications of this separation

### Implication 1: "Software AI thesis" overstates thematic coherence

BakeMark (bakery distribution) and BioIVT (biospecimen supply) have nothing to do with AI. Zywave's distress is primarily financial engineering — the insurance AMS product itself may be working fine. Applying the "AI disrupts software private credit" thesis uniformly across all these names overstates the AI driver and understates how much of the problem is just 2020–2021 vintage LBO debt colliding with 525bps of Fed hikes.

The more precise thesis is: **"2020–2021 vintage LBOs at L+450–550 are structurally impaired by rate shock; AI-exposed software categories have an additional operating headwind on top."** These are two distinct risk populations with some overlap.

### Implication 2: Rate shock names may recover cleanly; AI names may not

The Kaseya and Finastra outcomes are instructive: both were rate-shock-primary names and both refinanced cleanly into the BSL market once the rate environment became manageable. Lenders were repaid at par or near par.

Medallia and Zendesk face a harder path. Even if SOFR rates decline materially, AI-driven revenue pressure could mean the enterprise value doesn't recover to support full debt repayment. The rate environment helps but doesn't fix the operating problem.

Zywave is the ambiguous case: primarily rate shock, but the 2027 maturity wall arrives before rates may decline enough. The resolution depends almost entirely on whether Clearlake/Aurora inject equity (rate-shock path) or whether business deterioration has eroded EBITDA enough that equity injection doesn't pencil.

### Implication 3: Recovery assumptions should differ by driver

| Driver | Likely recovery path | Recovery rate (qualitative) |
|--------|--------------------|-----------------------------|
| SOFR rate shock (primary) | Amend-and-extend, sponsor injection, eventual BSL refinancing as rates normalize | High — business is sound; recovery near par if extension accomplished |
| AI/operating deterioration | Debt-for-equity exchange; lenders become equity owners in a compressed-EV business | Lower — franchise value in AI-disrupted category is uncertain; Pluralsight template |
| HoldCo structural subordination | Holdco preferred writes off; OpCo debt recovers | OpCo debt near par (BioIVT 94.8¢); holdco equity zero |
| Mixed (both mechanisms) | Depends on which dominates; may require both operating fix and financial restructuring | Intermediate; Cornerstone/Zendesk are the live tests |

### Implication 4: FSK's PIK income problem is rate-shock-driven

FSK's Moody's Ba1 downgrade cited 14.7% PIK income share vs. 6.3% peer median. PIK income concentration is high when: (a) borrowers can't afford current cash interest (rate shock), so they PIK instead, and (b) FSK lent heavily into the 2020–2022 vintage at high-leverage, high-spread structures. The Medallia holding at FSK is primarily a rate-shock + operating problem. FSK's PIK concentration across the broader portfolio is primarily rate-shock-driven — many borrowers are PIKing because cash flow doesn't cover the doubled interest burden, not because they're in terminal distress.
