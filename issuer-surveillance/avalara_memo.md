# Avalara — issuer memo

## One-line thesis
Avalara is the **strongest control case for compliance-driven software defensibility** in this universe: filing-confirmed first-lien exposure at CGBD and OCSL shows clean par-or-better marks, supporting the view that tax and compliance automation is more resilient than discretionary or AI-vulnerable software categories — but the 2028 maturity wall creates a specific refinancing date to monitor.

## What the company does
Avalara provides tax-compliance automation software that helps businesses calculate, collect, report, and remit taxes across jurisdictions and transaction types. This is one of the more intuitively defensible software categories because:
- compliance workflows are **non-discretionary** — companies cannot choose not to collect taxes
- the software handles a task that grows more complex as businesses scale, adding SKUs, geographies, and channel types
- switching costs are high because compliance history, tax rules, and filing logic are deeply embedded in the workflow
- failure to maintain compliance creates legal and financial risk for customers, creating strong retention incentives

## Why it matters
- **confirmed private-credit financing** per Reuters-style reporting tied to the Vista Equity Partners take-private ($8.4 billion, 2022)
- **filing-confirmed exposure at CGBD and OCSL**, both at clean par-or-better marks
- Avalara is the clearest case in this universe where the "mission-critical" argument for software credit defensibility is most credible — making it the right control case against which to test whether AI risk, market cyclicality, or budget pressure affects more vulnerable categories first
- the 2028 maturity creates a near-term refinancing event that will test whether the compliance-software thesis holds in a post-2022 market

## Debt / financing timeline
- **2022:** Vista Equity Partners acquires Avalara in a transaction valued at approximately **$8.4 billion**.
- **2022:** Reuters-style public reporting indicates the deal was supported by **private-credit financing**, consistent with the broader wave of Vista take-privates funded through direct lending.
- **Q3–Q4 2025:** CGBD and OCSL both carry Avalara first-lien positions at or above par in filed schedules of investments. No PIK, no non-accrual.

## Why Avalara is the right control case

**The compliance-software thesis:**
Tax compliance automation is structurally different from customer-support, workflow, or analytics software in two important ways:
1. **The risk of non-performance falls on the customer** — a tax calculation error or missed filing creates penalties and audits, not just inconvenience. This creates stronger retention behavior than discretionary software.
2. **AI is not yet a substitute for tax compliance software** — large language models can assist with tax research but cannot safely replace deterministic tax rules engines across thousands of jurisdictions and transaction types. This is unlike customer-support or content-generation software where AI can take over workflow end-to-end.

If Avalara continues to show clean marks while names like Medallia or Zendesk deteriorate, it helps validate the thesis that **software credit risk is a dispersion story** with meaningful subcategory variation.

## Risks worth watching
Despite the strong defensive thesis, Avalara is not risk-free:

1. **Vista's operational approach** — Vista Equity Partners typically pushes hard on operating efficiency post-close. If cost reductions compromise product investment or customer service, retention can weaken.

2. **2028 maturity** — The October 2028 refinancing date is close enough to watch actively. A 2025–2026 refinancing would reveal current lender demand; delaying to 2028 under current marks is possible but not risk-free.

3. **Slower international expansion** — Avalara's value proposition requires deep jurisdiction-specific tax rules. International expansion is slower and more complex than domestic scaling, which can constrain growth trajectories.

4. **Competitive pressure from ERP integration** — SAP and Oracle both offer tax calculation modules that can reduce the standalone Avalara value proposition for companies already heavily invested in those ecosystems.

## Hidden-mark-risk thesis
The mark risk is **low relative to this universe** but not zero. The main scenario where Avalara marks could weaken:
- Vista dividend recap or aggressive capital structure optimization that increases leverage above original underwriting levels
- growth deceleration driven by ERP competitor pressure or market saturation in US SMB
- refinancing at 2028 maturity proves difficult or requires structural concessions if the market is less favorable than today

## Working judgment
- **Hidden mark risk: 2 / 5** — compliance-software defensibility is real; current marks are clean; main risk is latent refinancing
- **BDC bond relevance: 3 / 5** — CGBD and OCSL confirmed; useful control case for benchmarking software resilience
- **Recovery fragility: 2 / 5** — mission-critical compliance workflow with genuine switching costs reduces recovery risk materially vs. discretionary software
