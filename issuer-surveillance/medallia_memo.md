# Medallia — issuer memo

## One-line thesis
Medallia has crossed from mark-surveillance to active restructuring: lenders (led by Blackstone) **refused to extend PIK relief in early 2026**, forcing ~$100M in incremental annual cash interest against ~$200M EBITDA, while BXSL marks the first-lien at **77.75¢** and FSK faces a Moody's speculative-grade downgrade partly attributed to Medallia — the clearest case in the surveillance universe where private-credit stress has reached the restructuring decision point.

## What the company does
Medallia sells customer-experience and employee-experience software used to collect feedback, analyze behavior, and improve service and retention outcomes across enterprises. It was easy to finance because it fit the classic sponsor-backed software template:
- recurring revenue
- enterprise customers
- embedded workflows
- analytics and data stickiness narrative
- Thoma Bravo ownership with a strong software operational playbook

That same template is what makes the name so useful now. If a company like Medallia starts to wobble, the implication is not just "this borrower is weak." It is that recurring-revenue software underwriting may have offered less downside protection than lenders assumed.

## Why it matters
- large, publicly identified direct-lender group with Blackstone as lead
- filing-confirmed mark dispersion of 21 percentage points between two public BDC vehicles
- PIK tranche active at FSK (4.0% PIK), signaling cash-versus-accrual complexity
- soft-asset recovery profile with no meaningful hard collateral
- reported markdowns and mark drift across lenders from late 2024 through early 2026
- plausible path from issuer stress to broader software-book skepticism

## Debt / financing timeline
- **August 2021:** Medallia agrees to be acquired by Thoma Bravo in a transaction valuing the company at **$6.4 billion**.
- **August 2021:** Latham identifies the financing as the then-**largest recurring-revenue unitranche** structure, naming **Blackstone Alternative Credit Advisors** as lead lender and **Apollo, KKR credit arms, and Antares** as co-lenders. Original lender group: Blackstone (lead/agent), Apollo, KKR, Antares, HPS, Monroe Capital. Approximately **$1.8B at origination** (ABF Journal). Loan carries **cash spread 6.5% over SOFR with 4% PIK** — 4 points capitalized and added to principal rather than paid in cash.
- **Late 2024:** Public reporting places Blackstone's mark on the Medallia loan at approximately **94 cents**.
- **January 7, 2025:** Mark Bishof appointed **Chairman and CEO** (BusinessWire) — former Chief Business Officer of Qualtrics and CEO of Clarabridge. Third CEO since the 2021 take-private. Thoma Bravo's third attempt at a management team to drive the turnaround. Bishof also brought in Sid Banerjee (Clarabridge founder) as CSO and Bas Brukx as CFO.
- **Q1 2025:** Blackstone's mark drifts to approximately **89 cents** per public reporting.
- **February 2025:** Fitch **upgrades Qualtrics** (Silver Lake-backed) citing "significant improvement in profitability" and "industry-leading" positioning — suggesting market share is flowing toward Qualtrics at Medallia's expense.
- **June 2025:** Blackstone mark falls to approximately **87 cents** per public reporting.
- **Q3 2025:** Mark dispersion across lenders becomes visible in public reporting (Bloomberg, Nov 13, 2025 — "Apollo, KKR See Record-Wide Gap on Valuing Stressed Private Loan"): approximately **77 cents** at Apollo, **82 cents** at Blackstone, and **91 cents** at FS KKR. Bloomberg called this the **"largest gap yet in regulatory filings."**
- **October 2, 2025:** Medallia issues a press release claiming "demand growth of more than 30% year-over-year" for its AI-powered experience platform — marketing language, not recognized revenue. Bloomberg (April 2, 2026) subsequently describes "inability to generate consistent profitability."
- **Q4 2025 (10-K filings, December 31, 2025):** Filing-confirmed marks: **BXSL** marks at **78.3 cents** (cost $393.4M, FV $307.9M); **FSK** marks blended positions at approximately **~99 cents**, with a PIK tranche active (4.0% PIK at SOFR+250bps). BXSL's Q4 2025 mark trajectory: **94¢ → 89¢ → 87¢ → 82¢ → 77.75¢** (per BXSL Q4 earnings presentation).
- **February 25–26, 2026:** Q4 2025 earnings calls. **BXSL management** states the 77.75¢ mark "reflects a slower-than-expected turnaround under a new management team" and that **"discussions around the capital structure are expected."** FSK Q4 earnings call attributes **~$29M in Q4 unrealized losses** to Medallia; FSK's below-par tranche specifically disclosed at approximately **$185M FV / $233M cost (~79¢)**.
- **March 24, 2026:** Moody's **downgrades FSK (FS KKR Capital Corp) to Ba1** (speculative grade), stripping it of its investment-grade rating. Moody's cites FSK's **14.7% PIK income share** (vs. 6.3% peer median) and explicitly names Medallia as a large marked-down non-non-accrual as signals of earnings quality concern (Alternative Credit Investor; PitchBook).
- **April 2, 2026 (forcing event):** Bloomberg reports **lenders (led by Blackstone) refused to extend another PIK lifeline**. Shifting Medallia from PIK to full-cash-pay is estimated to add approximately **$100M per year to annual debt service**, bringing total annual interest obligations to ~$300M against ~$200M in EBITDA — an **interest coverage ratio of approximately 0.67x**. Total debt has grown to **nearly $3 billion** from ~$1.8B at origination via PIK accrual and add-on financing. Restructuring options under discussion: (1) debt-for-equity swap giving lenders control; (2) fresh equity injection from Thoma Bravo.
- **Status as of April 2026:** No formal restructuring announced. Active capital-structure negotiations between Thoma Bravo and the lender group led by Blackstone. No non-accrual designation at any major BDC holder as of Q4 2025 filings.

## Capital-structure relevance
The financing visibility here is unusually strong for private credit. Public sources tie the original acquisition financing to a lender group that matters directly for this project because it bridges issuer analysis to major private-credit platforms.

That matters more than the borrower itself. Medallia is useful because it is a **signal asset**:
- if this credit keeps slipping, what does that say about similar software loans?
- if marks diverge 21 points across holders, how much confidence should we place in any one lender's carrying value?
- if FSK's PIK election reflects payment flexibility rather than investor appetite for PIK yield, what does that imply for the underlying cash economics?
- if lenders need patience, amendments, or PIK-style flexibility, where else might that be happening quietly?

## Hidden-mark-risk thesis
The core blind spot is that a recurring-revenue software credit can look acceptable in quarterly marks **long after** exit value, refinancing flexibility, or franchise confidence has begun to erode.

BXSL's filed mark at 78 cents tells us what at least one sophisticated Blackstone-owned vehicle thinks this loan is worth. FSK's filed mark at 99 cents for comparable exposure tells us either that the positions differ structurally, or that mark-setting assumptions differ materially across lenders, or both. Either reading is informative for anyone assessing software private-credit risk across portfolios.

## Why it could be more dangerous than current marks imply

### 1) It is a soft-asset recovery story
If Medallia weakens, lenders are not relying on hard collateral. Recovery depends on preserving:
- customer retention and reference relationships
- product relevance in a market where AI is changing what "customer experience" software means
- engineering continuity
- buyer appetite in a distressed sale where franchise value can evaporate quickly

### 2) Software marks can lag business deterioration
The bearish case is not that Medallia is obviously insolvent today. It is that software-credit marks can stay too comfortable while:
- product momentum weakens
- execution issues build
- exit multiples compress
- refinancing flexibility deteriorates

BXSL's mark journey from approximately 100 to 78 over roughly 18 months illustrates exactly this dynamic.

### 3) PIK activity adds a layer of complexity
FSK's 4.0% PIK tranche means the loan balance is growing, not amortizing. If the borrower or lender is using PIK to manage cash burden, that is a different risk posture than a clean cash-pay first lien.

### 4) It is a read-through case for the whole software book
If a flagship recurring-revenue software credit backed by Blackstone, Apollo, and KKR — three of the largest direct lenders — is marked down to 78 cents by one major holder while marked at par by another, the question for any portfolio containing similar names is: whose mark is closer to right?

## First transmission channel to BDC unsecured bond risk
The first transmission channel is **portfolio-mark credibility**, not immediate default:
1. lower marks on visible lender portfolios erode NAV and reduce cushion above unsecured debt
2. weaker confidence in software underwriting quality raises uncertainty about similarly marked credits
3. higher perceived risk of future amendments or non-accrual migration signals unpredictability
4. wider spreads on BDC unsecured bonds if investors reassess whether asset quality metrics fully capture software-credit risk

## What could show up first if the story worsens
**Steps 1–3 have already occurred.** Updated sequence as of April 2026:
1. ~~Additional mark pressure / valuation drift~~ **COMPLETED**: BXSL 78¢, Apollo 77¢, trajectory deteriorating through Q4 2025
2. ~~Market commentary about underperformance~~ **COMPLETED**: Bloomberg Nov 2025 "record-wide gap" article, BXSL Q4 2025 earnings "turnaround slower than expected"
3. ~~Capital-structure discussions~~ **COMPLETED**: Bloomberg April 2, 2026 — lenders refuse PIK extension, restructuring negotiations underway
4. **NOW ACTIVE**: Formal restructuring event — debt-for-equity swap, fresh equity injection, or LME
5. Non-accrual designation at BDC holders (none as of Q4 2025 filings; could arrive in Q1 2026)
6. Realized losses recognized in BDC portfolios

**What the April 2026 forcing event means for BDC holders:** Lenders ending the PIK lifeline forces cash-pay at sub-1x coverage — this is a statement that lenders believe time and patience are no longer the resolution strategy. Either Thoma Bravo injects equity, or lenders take control via a debt-for-equity swap. Either outcome changes the credit profile materially. Non-accrual designation is a realistic next step if cash-pay obligations cannot be met.

## What would falsify or weaken the bearish view
The Medallia concern would fade if we saw:
- durable operating stabilization with evidence that churn and pricing remain healthy
- a refinancing or liability-management path that confirms lender confidence rather than forced patience
- BXSL marks recovering toward par as business conditions improve
- FSK's near-par marks proving to be accurate rather than lagging

## Working judgment
- **Hidden mark risk: 5 / 5** — filing-confirmed 21-point dispersion with ongoing drift
- **BDC bond relevance: 5 / 5** — directly confirmed in BXSL (Blackstone), FSK (FS KKR), and BCRED (Blackstone non-traded)
- **Recovery fragility: 5 / 5** — soft-asset, franchise-dependent, no hard collateral
