# Coupa — issuer memo

## One-line thesis
Coupa is an important calibration name for **enterprise spend-management software in the AI era**: a Thoma Bravo take-private with filing-confirmed BXSL first-lien exposure at clean par marks, useful primarily as a test of whether AI-enabled procurement tools create pressure on the traditional "mission-critical spend management" underwriting thesis.

## What the company does
Coupa sells business-spend management software covering procurement, expenses, invoicing, payments, and supply-chain workflows. It can look lender-friendly because procurement software is often embedded in enterprise operations — purchase orders, invoice approvals, supplier management, and expense reporting are daily activities that can be hard to disrupt once embedded.

But the category also depends on corporate spending intensity and organizational budget discipline. Coupa's core value proposition — enabling companies to manage and optimize what they spend — is directly tied to how aggressively enterprises are managing costs and supplier relationships. That creates a form of cyclicality: spend-management platforms can thrive when CFOs want tighter control, but can face slower growth when enterprise budgets are already constrained and incremental value from new software procurement is harder to justify.

## Why it matters
- **confirmed private-credit financing** per Thoma Bravo press releases and public reporting ($8.0 billion transaction, February 2023)
- **filing-confirmed first-lien exposure at BXSL** (Blackstone Secured Lending) — one of the highest-profile and most widely watched public BDC vehicles
- current marks are clean at par or slight premium
- Coupa is the best name in this universe for testing whether AI-enabled procurement tools (including Coupa's own AI features and competitors) strengthen or undermine the traditional value proposition of spend-management platforms

## Debt / financing timeline
- **December 2022:** Thoma Bravo announces the acquisition of Coupa Software for approximately **$8.0 billion** (approximately $81 per share, all-cash).
- **February 2023:** Thoma Bravo completes the acquisition of Coupa Software. Private-credit financing for the take-private is indicated in Thoma Bravo press releases and public reporting (Thoma Bravo transaction pages S9).
- **Q4 2025 (10-K):** BXSL files its schedule of investments confirming first-lien Coupa Software exposure at clean par-plus marks. No PIK, no non-accrual. Maturity February 2030.

## How to use Coupa in the broader analysis

### The spend-management thesis
Procurement and spend software occupies a specific risk position in this universe. On the defensive side:
- purchase approvals and invoice routing are daily operational necessities that cannot easily be interrupted
- multi-year enterprise contracts with automatic renewal are common in this category
- Coupa's embedded position in complex supplier-management workflows creates real switching costs

On the risk side:
- AI-native procurement tools are emerging that can automate parts of the Coupa workflow
- enterprise budgets for new software platforms face heightened scrutiny in a slower growth environment
- Thoma Bravo's post-close cost optimization can pressure product investment if growth slows

### How AI affects the thesis
Coupa launched Coupa AI and embedded predictive analytics features, but the category faces a structural question: does AI *expand* the value of spend-management software (by enabling better supplier risk analysis, demand forecasting, and maverick-spend detection), or does AI *compress* it (by enabling internal finance and procurement teams to automate the most labor-intensive workflows without an enterprise platform)?

This is the same question facing Zendesk, but the mechanism differs. Zendesk's AI risk is more about customer-support volume being automated end-to-end. Coupa's AI risk is more about incremental value from additional software features being harder to justify at existing contract prices.

### 2030 maturity context
The February 2030 maturity gives Coupa more runway than Zendesk (November 2028) or Avalara (October 2028). A 2030 maturity refinancing is far enough out that current marks accurately reflect today's risk view, not near-term refinancing pressure.

## Hidden-mark-risk thesis
The mark risk is **low currently** but the latent risk is real:
1. underwritten at peak 2022–2023 software valuations in a deal that closed at the start of a rate-rising environment
2. AI-native procurement tools could pressure Coupa's pricing power and renewal rates before the 2030 maturity
3. if Thoma Bravo's portfolio optimization thesis requires Coupa to carry higher leverage for longer, the refinancing flexibility at 2030 may be more constrained than current par marks imply

## Working judgment
- **Hidden mark risk: 3 / 5** — clean current marks; AI procurement pressure is real but not near-term; 2030 maturity provides runway
- **BDC bond relevance: 3 / 5** — BXSL confirmed; OTF confirmed via portfolio page; useful calibration case for Blackstone's tech software underwriting
- **Recovery fragility: 3 / 5** — spend-management software has real switching costs but is not immune to budget pressure or AI disruption
