# Zendesk — issuer memo

## One-line thesis
Zendesk is one of the cleanest **AI-risk software credit** cases in the set: the largest direct-lending LBO in history at the time of close, with filing-confirmed exposure across BXSL, FSK, BCRED, and Blue Owl Technology Finance — making it one of the few names in this universe where category-level disruption risk can be tracked directly through public BDC marks.

## What the company does
Zendesk provides customer-support and customer-service software, including ticketing, workflow automation, knowledge-base tools, and AI-enabled support products. It has historically looked easy to finance because customer-support systems are often embedded in daily operations and linked to high-volume workflow.

But the category also sits unusually close to AI-driven automation risk, which makes it useful for surveillance. The issue is not that AI automatically breaks the business tomorrow. The issue is that customer-support software may be one of the places where pricing pressure, seat compression, and product substitution appear earlier than lenders expect.

## Why it matters
- **largest direct-lending leveraged buyout in history at the time** ($10.2 billion, November 2022)
- **largest-ever ARR financing** at close, per Latham & Watkins
- filing-confirmed exposure across four public/semi-public BDC vehicles: BXSL, FSK, BCRED, and Blue Owl Technology Finance (OTF)
- customer-support software sits unusually close to AI-enabled workflow automation risk
- potentially useful bridge from software-category disruption into lender marks — if AI compresses the Zendesk value proposition, marks should follow

## Debt / financing timeline
- **June 2022:** Latham advises on financings supporting Zendesk's **$10.2 billion** acquisition by the investor group led by Hellman & Friedman and Permira, including the **largest-ever ARR financing** and the **largest direct-lending leveraged buyout in history** at the time.
- **November 2022:** Zendesk confirms the acquisition closes at approximately **$10.2 billion** (all-cash).
- **Q4 2022 / 2023:** ARCC reports a funded and unfunded first-lien Zendesk position in public portfolio materials, giving the name direct public-BDC relevance.
- **2022–2023:** Public reporting and project research identify the lender group as including **Blackstone, Apollo, Blue Owl, and HPS** as major direct lenders.
- **2026-01-31:** BCRED portfolio materials confirm **Zendesk, Inc.** as a first-lien senior secured investment.
- **2025–2026 (10-K/10-Q filings):** BXSL and FSK both carry Zendesk exposure in their filed schedules of investments. Blue Owl Technology Finance (OTF) also lists Zendesk as a first-lien senior secured loan in its public portfolio holdings.
- **2023–2026:** The broader investment case shifts toward whether customer-support software is more vulnerable to AI-driven pricing pressure, seat compression, and product substitution than generic software underwriting assumed.

## Capital-structure relevance
Zendesk matters because it is not just a software name in the abstract. It is a very large financed transaction with direct-lending relevance and a category-level question attached to it.

If customer-support software becomes more vulnerable to AI compression than lenders expected, Zendesk could become an early signal that:
- recurring-revenue underwriting was too generic
- software marks are slow to incorporate category-specific disruption risk
- lenders may need to reassess recovery assumptions for customer-experience and support software

The combination of filing-confirmed exposure across Blackstone (BXSL, BCRED), FS KKR (FSK), Blue Owl (OTF), and Ares (ARCC) means any mark changes here would be visible across multiple public disclosure channels simultaneously.

## Hidden-mark-risk thesis
Marks may lag because revenue can look stable for a while **before** AI-driven pricing pressure, seat compression, or weaker product differentiation shows up clearly in debt-market risk pricing.

The category risk is specific: Zendesk's core product — managing and routing customer support interactions — is precisely the workflow that AI-native tools are targeting most aggressively. If enterprise customers reduce headcount in support functions and automate more tickets, Zendesk faces both a seat compression risk and a product-differentiation challenge against newer AI-native entrants. The lender question is whether that pressure shows up in retention, NRR, or pricing before the 2028 maturity.

## Why it could matter to BDC unsecured bonds
For BDC bond investors, Zendesk matters because it can reveal that "software" was underwritten too generically. If customer-support software ends up being more vulnerable to AI disruption than lenders assumed, similar marks across visible BDC and private-credit portfolios may need to move.

## First transmission channel to BDC unsecured bond risk
The first transmission channel is likely **category-level software repricing**, via:
1. weaker confidence in customer-support software underwriting
2. lower marks or more cautious carrying values on similar software credits
3. concern that category disruption is not yet reflected in broader software books
4. wider BDC unsecured bond spreads if investors think lender marks lag real risk in AI-sensitive categories

## What to watch
- evidence of AI-driven pricing pressure or seat compression in Zendesk's product categories
- whether Zendesk's own AI strategy (Zendesk AI, Sunshine platform) strengthens the moat or simply narrows the gap with AI-native competitors
- refinancing behavior as the 2028 maturity approaches — terms will reveal market confidence
- any divergence between product optimism in press materials and mark behavior at BXSL or FSK
- whether ARCC's disclosed Zendesk position changes in size or accrual status in subsequent filings
- Blue Owl Technology Finance's Zendesk mark as an additional check point (OTF tends to concentrate in large tech LBOs)

## AI product and category timeline
- **2022–2023 (acquisition period):** Zendesk acquires Momentive (partially) and invests in automation tools; positioned as AI-enabled at close.
- **2023–2024:** Competitive pressure intensifies from AI-native customer support tools (e.g., Intercom with Fin, Salesforce Einstein, and pure-play LLM-native entrants).
- **2025–2026:** Customer-support software broadly faces the question of whether AI reduces agent headcount (reducing seat count and revenue for workflow tools) or whether AI augments agents and expands the value proposition. Zendesk's ability to answer this question favorably is the central credit thesis for lenders.
- **Note:** The financing side of this timeline is well-evidenced. The AI-product and pricing evidence needs further development with dated competitive and operating data.

## What would falsify or weaken the bearish view
The Zendesk concern would soften if we saw:
- durable evidence that Zendesk's AI integration improves competitive positioning rather than compressing it
- healthy pricing and retention despite category-level disruption
- refinancing terms as the 2028 maturity approaches that validate lender confidence
- little sign of mark pressure despite closer scrutiny of AI-sensitive software credits

## Working judgment
- **Hidden mark risk: 4 / 5** — clean current marks but category-level AI risk is real and underappreciated
- **BDC bond relevance: 4 / 5** — the most filing-confirmed BDC visibility of any single name in the universe (BXSL, FSK, BCRED, OTF, ARCC)
- **Recovery fragility: 4 / 5** — soft-asset recovery dependent on franchise value in a category under AI pressure
