# Zendesk — Issuer Surveillance Memo

## One-line thesis
Zendesk is a pure private-credit deal — no BSL, no CLO float, no secondary market price anchor — with ~$430M annual cash interest against ~$400–500M EBITDA (~1x coverage), a 52% markdown on ARCC's preferred equity in a single quarter, and the largest single holder (Blackstone BCRED, ~$1B+ estimated) entirely invisible to public market investors.

## What the company does
Zendesk provides customer-support and service software: ticketing, workflow automation, knowledge-base tools, and AI-enabled support products. Revenue ~$2.0B (FY2025, estimated). The core product sits directly in the path of AI-native workflow automation — customer-support is one of the first enterprise functions where LLM-powered agents are replacing human workflows.

**Revenue trajectory (private since November 2022):**

| Period | Revenue | Growth |
|--------|---------|--------|
| FY2022 (last public year) | ~$1.60B | ~25% YoY |
| FY2023 | ~$1.75B | ~10% |
| FY2024 | ~$1.93B | ~10% |
| FY2025 | ~$2.01B | ~4% |

Growth has decelerated from 25%+ pre-LBO rates to mid-single digits — precisely the pattern recurring-revenue underwriting models assume away. AI ARR was ~$200M exiting 2025 (targeting $400–500M for 2026), but outcome-based "per resolution" pricing at $1.50–2.00/ticket creates downward average-revenue pressure as automation scales.

## Why it matters
- **Largest direct-lending LBO in history at the time** ($10.2B, November 2022)
- **No BSL — entire $4.6B first-lien is private credit**, held by 5 institutional lenders; no CLO float, no secondary market pricing, no external price anchor
- **BCRED (Blackstone's non-traded credit fund, ~$83B AUM) is the largest single holder** — entirely opaque to public market investors
- **~1x interest coverage**: ~$430M annual cash interest (S+500 on $4.6B at SOFR ~4.3%) vs. ~$400–500M estimated EBITDA
- **ARCC preferred equity down 52%** in Q3→Q4 2025 ($27.9M → $13.4M, common to −$2M) — first concrete mark signal; first-lien still clean at par
- **November 2028 first-lien maturity** — runway until late 2028, but 1x coverage means no de-leveraging without either operating improvement or equity injection

**Annual interest burden: ~$430M** (SOFR ~4.3% + 500bps on $4.6B funded). This is the dominant credit risk metric. At estimated EBITDA of $400–500M, coverage is approximately 0.9–1.2x — the thinnest in the surveillance universe outside Medallia.

**Q3 2024 repricing from S+650 → S+500**: This 150bps reduction occurred as the broader leveraged loan market repriced in mid-2024. It could signal lender confidence (they gave up spread), or it may reflect a concession negotiation where lenders accepted lower yield in exchange for other terms. Either interpretation is valid; what matters is the company still has first-lien debt service of ~$430M/year.

**Key structural insight:** The five-lender club holds ~$4.6B in senior debt. The BDC-visible slices (ARCC ~$36M, OBDC ~$178M, GBDC ~$28M, FSK ~$159M, BXSL ~$2M, TCPC ~$6M) total ~$409M — approximately **9% of total first-lien exposure**. The other ~$4.2B is in non-traded or institutional vehicles with no public disclosure. BCRED alone may hold $1B+.

**This is the inverse of the Cornerstone/Zywave structure.** In those deals, the BSL TLB ($2.1B / $450M) was in CLOs with public pricing. Here, all debt is private — there's no external price anchor at all. When ARCC marks its preferred at 52% down, there's no BSL market to validate or contradict it. The marks are all you have.

The preferred stock is carried at FV significantly above original cost (PIK accretion has been growing the balance), which means even a 52% markdown still leaves FV above the small initial cost basis. What the 52% markdown signals is that the *rate of accreted value* has reversed — ARCC now believes the preferred equity recovery has materially diminished.

## Coverage math — the critical number

At SOFR ~4.3% + 500bps = **~9.3% all-in rate** on $4.6B:
- Annual cash interest: **~$428M**
- Estimated EBITDA: **~$400–500M** (derived from revenue ~$2.0B at ~22% margin)
- Coverage: **~0.9–1.2x** — the razor's edge

For comparison:
- Medallia: 0.67x (confirmed distress)
- Cornerstone: ~1.2–1.5x estimated (unknown exact EBITDA)
- Zywave: historically negative FCF per S&P 2023

If Zendesk's AI-competitive revenue pressure drives EBITDA margin compression from ~22% to ~18–19%, coverage drops to ~0.75–0.85x — Medallia territory. This is the core credit risk.

Zendesk's response — $200M AI ARR (exiting 2025), Klaus acquisition, per-resolution pricing — is credible but the unit economics of per-resolution pricing create an inherent tension: if AI improves, revenue per seat goes down.

## Monitoring thesis

**The key transmission path from credit quality to BDC marks has no public price anchor.** Unlike Cornerstone (where the BSL TLB at 78–83¢ leads the BDC marks), Zendesk has no secondary market observable. The only early warning signals are:
1. ARCC's quarterly preferred equity mark — the 52% markdown is a leading indicator
2. Any BCRED disclosure in non-traded fund reports (rare)
3. Operational news: revenue disclosures (from H&F/Permira portfolio reports), competitive announcements, headcount data

**The 2028 maturity creates a different dynamic than Cornerstone's 2026 revolver.** Zendesk has approximately 2.5 years before the first refinancing test. In that window, either: (a) AI revenue ($400–500M ARR target) validates the LBO thesis and lenders refinance at improved terms, or (b) operating deterioration compounds and lenders face a $4.6B refinancing of a stressed credit in 2028.

## Working judgment
- **Hidden mark risk: 5 / 5** — no BSL secondary price, BCRED opaque; the entire 91% of 1L exposure has no public price signal; ARCC preferred at −52% is the only concrete mark data point
- **BDC bond relevance: 5 / 5** — broadest private-credit BDC visibility; $10.2B LBO; FSK, OBDC, ARCC, GBDC all confirmed; ~1x coverage is the core risk
- **Recovery fragility: 4 / 5** — customer-support software under AI pressure; franchise value uncertain; $4.6B refinancing in 2028 requires demonstrating growth trajectory
