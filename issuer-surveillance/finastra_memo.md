# Finastra — issuer memo

## One-line thesis
Finastra is a **refinancing and operating-surveillance priority** in the software private-credit universe — the largest single U.S. private-credit deal ever at time of close ($5.3 billion, September 2023), carrying through to a 2029 maturity wall with filing-confirmed exposure across five BDC/credit vehicles and a 4-point mark dispersion that reflects rate structure rather than credit stress.

## What the company does
Finastra is a large financial-software vendor serving banks and other financial institutions across core banking, payments, lending, treasury, and capital-markets workflows. Formed from the 2017 merger of Misys and D+H Financial Technology, and backed by Vista Equity Partners since 2012. The credit screens well on paper:
- mission-critical customer workflows embedded in daily banking operations
- recurring software and maintenance revenue
- high switching costs (core banking changes take years to execute)
- global scale across more than 8,000 financial institutions
- Vista Equity Partners backing with a track record of software operationalization

The category risk is that banking-software capex is highly correlated with bank profitability and IT budget cycles. A prolonged banking-sector slowdown reduces Finastra's ability to grow, upsell, and offset churn — all of which matter at this leverage level.

## Why it matters
- **scale**: at $5.3 billion, this is among the largest single private-credit loans ever made; any credit movement is market-relevant
- **multi-vehicle visibility**: filing-confirmed across ARCC, OCSL, OBDC (revolving + term), and GBDC, plus portfolio-page confirmed at OTF
- **2029 maturity**: the September 2029 refinancing window is part of the broader concentrated maturity wall across this software universe
- **calibration case**: Finastra tests whether banking-IT software — which looks more defensive than pure-SaaS — holds up under sustained leverage

## Debt / financing timeline
- **September 2023:** Finastra completes a **$5.3 billion** private-credit refinancing — at the time the **largest private-credit deal in the U.S.** The package includes a **$4.82 billion senior secured unitranche term loan** and a **$500 million senior secured multicurrency revolving credit facility**, with more than twenty lenders and borrowers across multiple jurisdictions (source: Kirkland & Ellis).
- **September 2023:** OHA (Oak Hill Advisors) discloses acting as lead arranger, committing more than **$800 million**, and framing the refinancing as taking out maturing syndicated first- and second-lien debt while deleveraging the company.
- **2025:** Public reporting indicates Finastra launched a syndicated refinancing process to take out part of the 2023 private-credit stack — execution of this process is a key surveillance catalyst for understanding lender demand and market pricing.
- **Q3–Q4 2025:** All confirmed BDC holders carry Finastra positions at or near par with no non-accrual designations. ARCC marks its floating-rate position at 102.3 cents; OCSL marks its fixed-rate position at 98.3 cents — a dispersion explained by rate structure, not credit quality.

## The 4-point mark dispersion: rate structure, not credit quality

The most analytically notable data point in the Finastra filing set is the 4-point gap between ARCC (102.3 cents) and OCSL (98.3 cents). This warrants explanation rather than alarm:

- **ARCC holds a floating-rate first-lien loan** at approximately SOFR + spread, generating current cash yield around 10.97% (Q4 2025)
- **OCSL holds a fixed-rate first-lien loan** at 7.25% — a below-market coupon in a high-rate environment

In a SOFR + spread environment where equivalent floating-rate yields are 10-11%, a 7.25% fixed-rate loan trades at a discount to par to compensate for the below-market coupon. This is a standard interest rate duration effect, not a credit concern.

If OCSL's fixed-rate Finastra loan were marked at 102 cents (matching ARCC), that would imply the market values a 7.25% fixed-rate loan the same as a 11% floating-rate loan on the same borrower — which makes no sense. The 98.3-cent mark on a 7.25% fixed loan reflects the appropriate discount.

**Conclusion:** The ARCC–OCSL dispersion does not indicate two lenders seeing the same credit differently. It indicates two lenders holding structurally different instruments on the same borrower, and the marks reflect those differences correctly.

## What the filing data does and does not tell us
The filing data confirms:
- At least five lenders (ARCC, OCSL, OBDC, GBDC, OTF) hold first-lien Finastra exposure as of Q4 2025
- All confirmed positions are accruing (no non-accrual at any confirmed holder)
- Marks are in the 98–102 range, broadly consistent with a performing credit
- ARCC's multi-entity obligor structure (Finastra USA, DH Corporation/Societe DH, Finastra Europe) suggests the ARCC credit agreement covers the combined global entity; other lenders may hold exposure solely to the U.S. obligor

What the filing data does not tell us:
- Operating performance at the borrower level (ARR growth, EBITDA, leverage ratio)
- Outcome or terms of the 2025 syndicated refinancing attempt
- Whether any lender has signaled private concerns or shadow-marked positions below public schedules
- Sponsor (Vista Equity Partners) behavior or willingness to inject capital

## Capital-structure relevance
Finastra matters because of its size. A $5.3 billion private-credit loan cannot be dismissed as idiosyncratic. If:
- operating performance deteriorates materially, or
- the 2029 refinancing market proves less receptive than the 2023 private-credit market, or
- Vista Equity Partners seeks to recap or de-lever through a liability management transaction

...then the read-through to BDC marks would be material across all confirmed holders. At ARCC alone, $52.6M of first-lien Finastra exposure (current FV) represents a meaningful single-name position that would require disclosure of any mark change.

## Hidden-mark-risk thesis
Current marks are clean across all confirmed holders, but the latent risk vectors are real:

1. **Scale and leverage**: $5.3 billion of first-lien debt on a financial-software platform is a large absolute leverage load. Banking-software revenue is more stable than discretionary SaaS, but it is not immune to IT budget pressure or banking-sector consolidation
2. **Refinancing concentration**: September 2029 is part of the same maturity cluster (Kaseya, Anaplan, Pluralsight, Finastra) that will all be refinancing within months of each other — lender supply/demand dynamics will matter
3. **Fintech disruption risk**: Cloud-native banking platforms (Thought Machine, Mambu, Temenos cloud) and large-bank in-house modernization represent slow-moving but real competitive pressure on Finastra's installed base
4. **Fixed-rate position duration**: OCSL's 7.25% fixed-rate position is below current market rates and will remain so until rates fall materially — this is a drag on OCSL's income relative to floating-rate alternatives

## Working judgment
- **Hidden mark risk: 2 / 5** — all confirmed marks are clean; rate-structure dispersion is explained; no non-accrual anywhere; main risk is forward-looking (leverage, 2029 refi, fintech disruption)
- **BDC bond relevance: 4 / 5** — size alone makes it market-relevant; confirmed across five vehicles; the 2029 maturity is close enough to watch
- **Recovery fragility: 2 / 5** — banking software with genuine switching costs and global scale has reasonable recovery dynamics relative to pure-SaaS; Vista backing adds a layer of optionality

## Parser artifact note — OBDC $93.5M entry
The portfolio parser flags a $93.5M OBDC entry for "Finastra USA, Inc." as non-accrual in both Q3 and Q4 2025. This entry has three diagnostic signatures of a subtotal row rather than a discrete loan: (1) empty rate_str, par_str, and pct_nav_str — formatting consistent with a totals row; (2) fair value above cost (100.9 cents) — inconsistent with any genuine non-accrual, which would imply a FV discount; and (3) **identical cost ($93,496K) and FV ($94,335K) across both Q3 and Q4 quarter-ends** — real loans change quarter to quarter. The non-accrual flag was applied via the parser's issuer-level carry-forward (`_tbl_na`), not from a direct row-level footnote match on this entry. The actual OBDC Finastra positions (revolving, term loan) are captured separately and show no non-accrual. This is a known pattern in iXBRL SOI parsing where subtotal rows inherit footnote flags from the same-issuer rows above them in the table.
