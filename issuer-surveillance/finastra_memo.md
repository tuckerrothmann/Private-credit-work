# Finastra — issuer memo

## One-line thesis
Finastra is the most important **software private-credit refinancing and credit-quality bellwether** in the set: one public BDC vehicle has already placed its Finastra term loan on **non-accrual**, while another carries the same-maturity exposure at a slight premium to par — a split reading that raises serious questions about consistency of marks across the private-credit ecosystem.

## What the company does
Finastra is a large financial-software vendor serving banks and other financial institutions across core banking, payments, lending, treasury, and capital-markets workflows. In lender terms, it screens well on paper because it appears to offer:
- mission-critical customer workflows
- recurring software and maintenance revenue
- high switching costs
- global scale
- enterprise-software margins and Vista Equity Partners backing

That is exactly why the name matters. If a company with these characteristics has a term loan on non-accrual at a major BDC even while held at premium by another, investors should question how consistent private-credit marks are across the broader software universe.

## Why it matters
- **largest-ever U.S. private-credit deal at the time** ($5.3 billion, September 2023)
- filing-confirmed non-accrual position at OBDC, a major public BDC
- divergent marks across OBDC (non-accrual), ARCC (slight premium), and OCSL (near par)
- scale makes it systemically important: any mark pressure or credit deterioration here is too large to treat as idiosyncratic
- Vista-backed software issuer in a category that sounds defensive but depends heavily on banking-sector capex cycles

## Debt / financing timeline
- **September 2023:** Finastra completes a **$5.3 billion** private-credit refinancing, described at the time as the **largest private-credit deal in the U.S.** The package includes a **$4.82 billion senior secured unitranche term loan** and a **$500 million senior secured multicurrency revolving credit facility**, with more than twenty lenders and borrowers across multiple jurisdictions (source: Kirkland & Ellis).
- **September 2023:** OHA (Oak Hill Advisors) discloses acting as lead arranger, committing more than **$800 million**, and framing the refinancing as taking out maturing syndicated first- and second-lien debt while deleveraging the company.
- **2025:** Public reporting indicates Finastra launched a syndicated refinancing process to take out part of the 2023 private-credit stack — making refinancing execution a key surveillance catalyst.
- **By Q3 2025:** OBDC places its first-lien Finastra term loan **on non-accrual** while still carrying it at approximately par in fair value.
- **Q3–Q4 2025:** ARCC carries its comparable Finastra first-lien exposure at a slight **premium to cost** (no non-accrual); OCSL also carries its position near par (no non-accrual).

**Critical finding:** OBDC's $93.5 million first-lien Finastra term loan has been on **non-accrual** in both Q3 2025 (10-Q) and Q4 2025 (10-K) filings. The position is carried near par in fair value, suggesting OBDC views the collateral as recoverable but has stopped recognizing interest income — a meaningful credit signal that does not yet appear to be reflected in ARCC's or OCSL's marks or accrual status for the same borrower.

**Mark and accrual divergence:**
- OBDC: non-accrual on term loan, revolver still accruing
- ARCC: accruing normally, marked at 102 cents (slight premium)
- OCSL: accruing normally, marked at 98 cents (near par)

## Capital-structure relevance
Finastra matters because it is large enough to function as a **market-clearing event**. Small or messy credits can be dismissed as idiosyncratic. A software loan of this size cannot.

The non-accrual finding at OBDC raises specific questions:
- Was there a missed or deferred interest payment that triggered non-accrual treatment?
- If OBDC stopped accruing while ARCC continues to accrue, which view is correct?
- Does the FV-near-par non-accrual position reflect genuine recovery confidence or a mark that has not yet caught up to credit-stress recognition?
- If the 2025 syndicated refinancing attempt did not fully execute, what risk remains with private-credit holders?

## Hidden-mark-risk thesis
The blind spot is two-layered: **size and reputation postpone recognition**, and **divergent accrual treatment across lenders suggests the credit is in a more uncertain state than any single holding might imply**. ARCC at 102 cents and OBDC with a non-accrual — both holding first-lien Finastra exposure maturing September 2029 — cannot both be fully right.

## Why it could matter to BDC unsecured bonds
The first-order issue is not immediate credit collapse. It is the **information content of divergent treatment**:
- does OBDC's non-accrual reflect a temporary technical event, or is it signaling something ARCC's marks do not yet acknowledge?
- does the market refinance the credit cleanly via syndication, or does difficult risk remain with existing direct lenders?
- if lenders need structural concessions, the read-through can pressure marks and sentiment across BDC books with meaningful software exposure

## First transmission channel to BDC unsecured bond risk
The likely first transmission channel is **mark and underwriting-credibility reassessment**, via:
1. OBDC's non-accrual creates a factual anchor that the credit is not performing cleanly
2. if ARCC ultimately follows with an accrual change, it signals that large, diversified BDC platforms are not immune to software private-credit stress
3. weaker confidence in large-software underwriting across portfolios — if Finastra's $5.3B loan has accrual problems, what else in the private-credit software book is quietly less healthy than marks suggest?
4. wider BDC unsecured bond spreads if investors price in lagged mark recognition and weaker recovery assumptions across similar credits

## What to watch
- whether OBDC's non-accrual on the Finastra term loan resolves (via cure, restructuring, or write-off) or persists
- whether ARCC transitions to non-accrual treatment as the credit situation evolves
- 2025 syndicated refinancing terms: what did the market take, what was left behind, and at what pricing?
- any evidence of amendments, maturity extensions, or PIK elections on the outstanding private-credit stack
- Vista Equity Partners' behavior — sponsor support or strategic optionality?
- whether OCSL's smaller position shows any change in accrual or mark treatment in subsequent filings

## What would falsify or weaken the bearish view
The Finastra concern would ease if we saw:
- OBDC's non-accrual resolved through a cured payment or a clean restructuring that confirms lender recovery
- a broad, well-executed syndicated refinancing that validates prior marks rather than challenging them
- evidence of steady deleveraging and durable operating performance at the borrower level
- ARCC's continued above-par carry proving to be the more accurate read as conditions stabilize

## Working judgment
- **Hidden mark risk: 4 / 5** — filing-confirmed non-accrual at OBDC vs. premium marks at ARCC is the clearest multi-vehicle divergence in the universe
- **BDC bond relevance: 5 / 5** — size alone, plus confirmed cross-BDC exposure with different accrual treatment
- **Recovery fragility: 4 / 5** — first-lien financial software with decent collateral, but scale and accrual divergence increase uncertainty
