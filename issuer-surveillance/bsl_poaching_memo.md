# BSL "Poaching Back" — Private Credit Refinancing via Syndicated Markets (2025)

*The reversal of the 2020–2023 private-credit market-share trend: broadly syndicated loan (BSL) markets are recapturing the largest and cleanest private-credit borrowers, selectively extracting the best assets from BDC portfolios and leaving behind a higher-concentration remainder.*

---

## The core dynamic

Between 2020 and 2023, private credit captured enormous market share from BSL as volatility, rate uncertainty, and the speed of direct-lending execution made private credit the preferred financing route for large sponsor-backed LBOs. Many deals that would ordinarily have been BSL transactions went to private credit.

In 2025, the flow reversed. With BSL markets more receptive, and with private-credit spreads compressed significantly from their 2022–2023 peaks, the borrowers that qualified for BSL migrated back — locking in lower spreads, longer maturities, and the deeper capacity of the syndicated market.

**The critical asymmetry**: BSL does not take back all private-credit borrowers. It takes back the best ones. What remains in private-credit portfolios after a wave of BSL takeouts is, by construction, a more concentrated pool of the smaller, more complex, and more operationally challenged names.

## What separates BSL-eligible credits from those that stay in private credit

S&P Global's Q4 2024 leveraged finance update (January 31, 2025) provides the most rigorous threshold analysis:

**BSL-eligible credits that successfully transitioned:**
- Median EBITDA: **$84M** — approximately **triple the overall US middle-market median ($30M)**
- Median revenue: **$454M**
- Transaction size: $400M–$1.3B first-lien term loans
- Key criteria: strong revenue growth, operational stability, significant deleveraging since original private-credit deal

**S&P's conclusion:** "The evidence suggests that the threshold to transition to the BSL market remains high, marking it viable for only a relatively select group."

**KBRA's framing** (Q3 2025 BDC Compendium): BSL pressure is concentrated in the **upper middle market and large corporate segment** (above ~$50M EBITDA). The core middle market (below $50M EBITDA) remains firmly in private credit.

**The adverse selection implication**: The 12 largest BSL takeouts in 2025 alone removed approximately **$9B from BDC portfolios** — all of those were the cleanest, highest-EBITDA, most-BSL-eligible names. What BDC portfolios retain is skewed toward the credits that could NOT qualify: smaller, more leveraged, more complex, or operationally challenged names.

## What this does to BDC portfolio quality

### 1. Spread compression

As private-credit managers competed to retain or originate the best borrowers, spreads compressed. The illiquidity premium (private credit spread over BSL) compressed from 200+ bps in 2022–2023 to approximately **130–150bps** by mid-2025 — near a post-2019 low. With less compensation for complexity and illiquidity, new originations in the upper middle market began to look less attractive on a risk-adjusted basis.

- **Average illiquidity premium (H2 2025):** ~115bps (Aviva Investors, Feb 2026) — above long-run average in absolute terms, but compressed from the 2022–2023 private-credit opportunity window
- **Direct lending vs. BSL delta:** ~147bps year-to-date mid-2025 — "tightest since 2019" (KBRA)

### 2. Portfolio quality divergence

After the BSL wave, the distribution of credit quality within private-credit portfolios has become more bimodal:

| Category | Examples | Status |
|----------|----------|--------|
| BSL-exit credits (paid off) | Kaseya (S+550→S+325), Finastra (S+725→S+400), KnowBe4, Alera | Gone — repaid at par or near-par |
| Surviving clean credits | Anaplan, Coupa, Avalara (partially) | Still in portfolios, marking near par |
| Watch-list credits | Zendesk (equity impaired −52%), Cornerstone (2L at 91¢) | Active surveillance |
| Stressed/restructuring credits | Medallia (77–78¢, PIK refusal), Pluralsight (completed workout) | Impairment confirmed |

### 3. FSK as the clearest structural casualty

Moody's March 24, 2026 downgrade of **FSK (FS KKR Capital Corp) to Ba1** (speculative grade) is the most tangible consequence of the adverse selection dynamic visible in a BDC's credit profile:

- FSK's **PIK income share: 14.7%** vs. **6.3% peer median** — the remaining portfolio skews toward companies that cannot fully service debt in cash
- Moody's explicitly flagged Medallia (large marked-down non-non-accrual) as an example of earnings quality concerns
- FSK's Q4 2025 non-accrual rate and PIK concentration suggest that as better credits left for BSL, FSK's remaining portfolio has a higher proportion of the stressed credits that did not qualify for BSL

**ARCC's contrarian response**: ARCC grew its portfolio **+$887M in 1H 2025** while OBDC contracted by $1.17B. ARCC's strategy — leaning into BSL volatility windows, taking positions in credits temporarily abandoned by the syndicated market — means ARCC is effectively providing the counter-cyclical capital when BSL pulls back. ARCC's portfolio averaged **10.6% yield on amortized cost** in Q3 2025 with non-accruals at only 1.8%.

## The surveillance thesis implication

**The poaching-back trend directly affects what remains in BDC portfolios and what is visible in BDC unsecured bond risk.**

Three compounding effects for BDC bond investors:

1. **NAV erosion from remaining stressed credits**: With BSL taking the outperformers, mark-to-market risk concentrates in the credits that cannot exit. Medallia (77¢ at BXSL), Cornerstone (91¢ second-lien at ARCC), and Zendesk (equity impaired) are the visible examples — but the logic says there are more.

2. **Prepayment-driven yield compression**: As the highest-spread private-credit loans (S+550+) are refinanced into BSL (S+325), the remaining portfolio yield compresses. BDC earnings per share are reduced, reducing dividend coverage and, over time, BDC bond coverage.

3. **Adverse selection accelerating**: The more visible software-credit stress becomes, the more investors scrutinize what remains, the wider BDC spreads go. This is the mechanism by which issuer-level credit stress transmits to BDC bond spread widening: not default, but portfolio-quality reassessment.

**The CNBC formulation** (March 25, 2026): "The composition of what remains in BDC portfolios has quietly worsened."

## BSL-eligible vs. private-credit-resident: the sorting criteria

| Characteristic | BSL-eligible | Stays in private credit |
|----------------|-------------|------------------------|
| EBITDA | >$50–100M | <$50M |
| Revenue | >$400M | variable |
| Leverage trajectory | Deleveraging since origination | Flat or increasing |
| Operational stability | Stable/improving ARR or EBITDA | Execution issues, AI disruption, cost pressure |
| Deal size | >$1B | Variable |
| Sponsor quality | Tier-1 sponsor with track record | All sponsors |
| AI disruption exposure | Low | Moderate to high (talent management, CX, analytics) |

**Key implication for the software surveillance universe**: Zendesk, Medallalia, and Cornerstone are exactly the credits where BSL eligibility is doubtful — large enough in absolute deal size, but with operating/competitive questions that make BSL investors reluctant. These credits cannot benefit from the spread savings that Kaseya and Finastra received. They stay in private credit, absorbing cumulative PIK accrual and mark deterioration, with the next exit options being distressed exchange, debt-for-equity, or a strategic sale.
