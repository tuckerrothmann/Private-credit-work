# Cornerstone OnDemand — issuer memo

## One-line thesis
Cornerstone OnDemand is the most advanced visible impairment case among AI-disrupted software credits: confirmed below-par marks across **both** the second-lien debt layer (91¢ at ARCC, 94¢ at OBDC) and the preferred equity layer (85¢ at ARCC, 89¢ at OBDC), with CGBD marking first-lien positions at 77–82¢ — suggesting this has already crossed from equity impairment into debt impairment across multiple instruments and multiple lenders.

## What the company does
Cornerstone OnDemand provides talent management software: learning management systems (LMS), performance management, recruiting, workforce planning, and skills/development tools used by HR and L&D organizations. It was one of the leading enterprise talent platforms before being taken private.

The category sits in a structurally challenged position:
- enterprise L&D and performance software was growing during the "talent war" era (2020–2022) but faces headcount pressure and AI substitution
- AI-native tools (Microsoft Viva, Workday Skills Cloud, and point solutions using LLMs) are increasingly capable of replicating core Cornerstone workflows
- corporates are cutting L&D budgets in a cost-discipline environment, directly pressuring seat counts and renewal rates
- consolidation of HR tech stacks means buyers are choosing all-in-one solutions (Workday, SAP SuccessFactors, ServiceNow) over best-of-breed platforms

## Why it matters
- **Clearlake Capital take-private at approximately $5.4 billion** (2022) — a large sponsor-backed LBO
- **Multi-BDC confirmed exposure**: ARCC, OBDC, CGBD, PNNT in filed Q4 2025 schedules
- **Both debt and equity marks are below par** — the impairment has already moved beyond the equity layer
- **ARCC originates new second-lien + preferred in Q4 2025** while marks are already discounted — signals ARCC pricing new exposure at a discount to earlier instruments
- This is the first name in the surveillance universe where BOTH the debt AND preferred equity marks show material discounts simultaneously

## Debt / financing timeline
- **2022:** Clearlake Capital acquires Cornerstone OnDemand for approximately **$5.4 billion** via leveraged buyout.
- **2022–2024:** Private-credit financing in place, including first-lien (ARCC, CGBD), second-lien (ARCC, OBDC), and preferred equity layers.
- **Q3 2025:** ARCC holds only a first-lien revolver ($8.4M FV at 7.27%). The large second-lien and preferred positions are not in Q3 ARCC filings — suggesting ARCC may have originated or funded these positions in Q4 2025.
- **Q4 2025:** ARCC adds $137.5M in second-lien + $179.2M in Series A preferred — new originations or funding of existing commitments — at marks below par (91.0¢ and 85.0¢ respectively). OBDC holds $229M in second-lien + preferred at similar marks. CGBD carries first-lien positions at 77–82¢.

## Capital-structure relevance
Cornerstone is more advanced in the impairment cycle than Zendesk:
- **Zendesk**: first-lien marks clean; only ARCC preferred equity impaired (−52%)
- **Cornerstone**: first-lien marks at CGBD are 77–82¢; second-lien at ARCC is 91¢ and OBDC 94¢; preferred at ARCC is 85¢ and OBDC 89¢

The impairment has crossed into the debt layers. The cross-sectional consistency (ARCC and OBDC marking second-lien at 91–94¢ independently) increases the credibility of the marks.

**ARCC's Q4 origination signal:** The fact that ARCC apparently originated or funded $316.7M in new second-lien + preferred during Q4 2025 — while already marking those positions below par — may indicate a distressed exchange or add-on financing at a discounted price. This pattern is consistent with a sponsor-led capital structure repair: new money comes in at a lower entry price in exchange for additional structural protections.

## Hidden-mark-risk thesis
The CGBD first-lien marks at 77–82¢ are the most alarming signal. In the Medallia case, the clearest stress indicator was a first-lien mark at BXSL of 78¢. Cornerstone's CGBD first-lien is in the same range, while PNNT holds the same first-lien at par — another mark dispersion requiring explanation.

If Cornerstone's talent management software is facing AI-driven pricing pressure and customer churn, the current marks may still lag the operating reality, as they did with Medallia and Zendesk.

## Why it could matter to BDC unsecured bonds
Cornerstone adds a confirmed multi-BDC impairment data point to the software category, directly alongside the AI disruption thesis. If enterprise L&D and talent management software is being compressed by AI tooling, Cornerstone is the most directly evidenced case in the current universe.

## What to watch
- OBDC: does the Cornerstone second-lien mark move below 90¢ in subsequent filings?
- CGBD: do the first-lien marks at 77–82¢ stabilize or continue declining?
- ARCC: does the Series A preferred ($152.3M FV) deteriorate further or does the new Q4 origination represent a reset at current marks?
- PNNT vs. CGBD: first-lien dispersion (par vs. 77¢) is similar to the Medallia/FSK-BXSL pattern — one of these is more right
- Any operational news: Clearlake portfolio announcements, management changes, customer wins/losses, Workday/SAP competitive displacement

## What would falsify the bearish view
- Stabilization of all debt and equity marks above current levels in subsequent filings
- Evidence of AI product integration improving Cornerstone's competitive positioning (AI-native LMS, skills mapping) rather than compressing its value proposition
- A clean refinancing or recapitalization at better economics

## Working judgment
- **Hidden mark risk: 4 / 5** — already showing first-lien impairment; CGBD 77–82¢ is the clearest signal; ARCC and OBDC second-lien marks at 91–94¢ confirm cross-lender consistency
- **BDC bond relevance: 4 / 5** — confirmed across ARCC, OBDC, CGBD, PNNT; ARCC's $316M new origination makes it a live Q4 2025 event
- **Recovery fragility: 4 / 5** — talent management software has moderate switching costs but is in the path of AI compression; no hard-asset recovery floor
