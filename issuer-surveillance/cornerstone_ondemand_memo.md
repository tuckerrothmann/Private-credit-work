# Cornerstone OnDemand — issuer memo

## One-line thesis
Cornerstone OnDemand is confirmed multi-layer impairment: both second-lien debt (91¢ at ARCC, 94¢ at OBDC) and preferred equity (85¢ at ARCC, 89¢ at OBDC) are below par, with the $2.1B broadly syndicated first-lien Term Loan B trading at approximately **78–83¢** in the secondary leveraged loan market — indicating the full capital structure, not just the private credit layers, has moved into distressed-zone pricing.

*Correction note (April 2026): Earlier versions of this memo incorrectly attributed CGBD first-lien marks at 77–82¢ and a PNNT first-lien at par to Cornerstone OnDemand (HR software). Those positions are actually **Cornerstone Building Brands** (vinyl siding, CD&R-backed) and **Cornerstone Advisors of Arizona** (financial consulting) respectively — different companies with similar names. The confirmed BDC holders of Cornerstone OnDemand (CSOD) are only ARCC and OBDC.*

## What the company does
Cornerstone OnDemand provides talent management software: learning management systems (LMS), performance management, recruiting, workforce planning, and skills/development tools used by HR and L&D organizations. It was one of the leading enterprise talent platforms before being taken private. Revenue: ~$830M TTM as of early 2026.

The category sits in a structurally challenged position:
- enterprise L&D and performance software was growing during the "talent war" era (2020–2022) but faces headcount pressure and AI substitution
- AI-native tools (Microsoft Viva, Workday Skills Cloud, and point solutions using LLMs) are increasingly capable of replicating core Cornerstone workflows
- corporates are cutting L&D budgets in a cost-discipline environment, directly pressuring seat counts and renewal rates
- consolidation of HR tech stacks means buyers are choosing all-in-one solutions (Workday, SAP SuccessFactors, ServiceNow) over best-of-breed platforms
- post-LBO acquisitions (SumTotal $200M Aug 2022; Talespin Mar 2024; SkyHive May 2024) suggest revenue is being bought rather than grown organically

## Why it matters
- **Clearlake Capital take-private at approximately $5.2–5.4 billion (October 2021)** — large sponsor-backed LBO
- **Multi-layer impairment confirmed by two independent BDC marks**: ARCC and OBDC both mark second-lien at 91–94¢ and preferred at 85–89¢
- **$2.1B BSL first-lien TLB trades at ~78–83¢** in the secondary leveraged loan market — distressed zone pricing on the senior-most instrument
- **October 2026 revolver maturity** — near-term wall for the $300M revolver; failure to extend would be a default trigger
- **ARCC originated $316M in new second-lien + preferred in Q4 2025** at already-discounted marks — stress financing signal

- **November 2025:** Second consecutive November layoff round.
- **July 2025:** S&P reaffirms 'B-', stable outlook.
- **Late 2025 / early 2026:** BSL first-lien TLB quoted at approximately **78–83¢** in secondary market.

## Capital-structure significance

**Cornerstone is more advanced in the impairment cycle than Zendesk:**
- **Zendesk**: first-lien marks clean at 99–103¢; only ARCC preferred equity impaired (−52%)
- **Cornerstone**: second-lien at 91–94¢ (ARCC + OBDC); preferred at 85–89¢; AND the $2.1B BSL first-lien trading at 78–83¢ in the public loan market

The BSL market pricing at 78–83¢ is the most important signal here. Unlike BDC marks (which are quarterly and subject to model assumptions), BSL secondary market quotes are real-time, arm's-length transaction prices from CLOs and loan fund managers. A $2.1B term loan trading at 78–83¢ means the market is pricing meaningful recovery risk on the senior-most instrument.

**ARCC's Q4 origination signal:** The fact that ARCC originated or funded $316M in new second-lien + preferred during Q4 2025 — at marks already below par — is consistent with a sponsor-led capital structure repair: new money from ARCC at discounted entry prices in exchange for structural seniority or covenant relief. ARCC's simultaneously appreciating common equity co-invest ($13.6M → $19.9M, +$6.3M) suggests the operating company itself still has positive enterprise value — the stress is in the over-levered capital structure, not the business.

**October 2026 revolver maturity** is the near-term forcing event. If Cornerstone cannot extend or refinance the $300M revolver before October 2026, it would constitute a default. Given the BSL TLB trading at 78–83¢, a revolver extension at current pricing may be expensive or unavailable on favorable terms. This is the analog to the Medallia PIK-refusal forcing event.

## Operational signals
- Two consecutive November layoff rounds (2024: ~10% workforce; 2025: additional round) — pattern of ongoing cost-cutting
- CEO Himanshu Palsule has reshuffled the leadership team repeatedly (executives averaging ~2 years)
- Revenue ~$830M TTM as of early 2026 — flat-to-modest growth since $5.2B LBO (steep valuation vs. scale)
- Bolt-on M&A (SumTotal, Talespin, SkyHive) suggests the company is buying revenue rather than growing organically
- AI-native HCM tools (Microsoft Viva, Workday Skills Cloud, SAP SuccessFactors) are structurally displacing best-of-breed LMS/performance software

## Hidden-mark-risk thesis
The BSL market pricing at 78–83¢ on the $2.1B first-lien TLB is the clearest stress indicator. Most BDC analysis focuses on the junior layers (second-lien and preferred visible in BDC filings), but the $2.1B first-lien — which is senior to everything ARCC and OBDC hold — is trading at meaningful distress. This is a total-enterprise-value problem, not just a junior-capital problem.

If Cornerstone's talent management software is facing AI-driven pricing pressure and customer churn, and if the $2.1B first-lien is already trading at 78–83¢, the second-lien (currently 91–94¢) and preferred (85–89¢) are likely to face additional compression in subsequent quarters.

## Why it could matter to BDC unsecured bonds
ARCC holds ~$354M cost basis in Cornerstone across multiple layers. At current blended mark (~90.5¢), that's ~$34M in unrealized losses. If the BSL market pricing (78–83¢) is predictive of where the full capital structure trades, ARCC's second-lien could decline from 91¢ toward 80¢ range, adding another ~$15–20M in unrealized losses. The preferred (at 85¢) is more vulnerable: if enterprise value deteriorates, the preferred absorbs next-dollar losses before the second-lien.

## What to watch
- **October 2026 revolver**: Will Clearlake/Cornerstone refinance or extend? Failure to do so is a default trigger.
- **BSL secondary market pricing**: Does the $2.1B TLB drop below 78¢ (toward distressed-exchange territory)?
- **ARCC Q1 2026 filing (May 2026)**: Does the second-lien mark hold at 91¢ or compress further? Does the preferred decline below 85¢?
- **OBDC Q1 2026**: Does OBDC's 93.8¢ second-lien converge toward ARCC's 91¢?
- **Layoffs and operational data**: A third consecutive November layoff round would confirm structural decline

## What would falsify the bearish view
- BSL TLB recovers to 90¢+ (would indicate credit markets see a refinancing path)
- October 2026 revolver extension completed at favorable terms
- Evidence of AI product integration improving Cornerstone's competitive position (AI-native LMS, skills mapping)
- A clean refinancing or recapitalization at better economics

## Working judgment
- **Hidden mark risk: 4 / 5** — BSL first-lien at 78–83¢ is the primary signal; ARCC and OBDC second-lien marks at 91–94¢ independently confirm junior layer stress; October 2026 revolver is a live forcing event
- **BDC bond relevance: 4 / 5** — $583M cost basis across ARCC + OBDC; ARCC's Q4 2025 new origination makes it a live Q4 2025 event
- **Recovery fragility: 4 / 5** — talent management software has moderate switching costs but is in the path of AI compression; no hard-asset recovery floor
